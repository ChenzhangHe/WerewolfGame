# Repeated Wolf self-explosions

The saved v4 three-round game contains 34 Wolf decisions across 11 interruption
windows: 31 passes and three explicit `action_explode` calls. Public explosions
were seats 7, 3, and 12, once each on days 1, 2, and 3. Native tool calls,
submitted actions, and referee events agree. No pass was converted into an
explosion, and the final action-only stage still offered both choices.

Each living Wolf receives an interruption opportunity before each permitted
speech. One Wolf choosing explosion ends that day's remaining discussion/exile
and creates another night opportunity. This explains the incentive and repeated
opportunities; it does not establish that sacrificing a Wolf is good strategy.

The model-authored private strategy notes described avoiding an expected exile
or protecting teammates. One note also planned future night cooperation after
self-sacrifice, contradicting permanent death. Notes are self-reports, not hidden
reasoning. These observations support a strategy/prompt investigation, not a
referee execution defect.

A confirmed prompt-design omission was found: the compact briefing did not
state win conditions or permanent-death consequences upfront. Both were behind
optional rule lookup, and the explosion action had a generic description.

New games use frozen protocol `player-tools-v5`. It supplies concise public win
and death facts in every briefing and neutral consequences on the explode/pass
tools. Explosion permanently eliminates the actor; dead Wolves do not join later
chat/attacks; the last Wolf dying gives Good victory. Passing only declines the
current interruption; another Wolf may still explode. Legal choices and referee
rules are unchanged. Old v1–v4 archives retain their original protocol.

Automated tests verify bilingual facts, both legal choices, generic Witch pass,
legacy manifest compatibility, restore, and exact archive audits. No new live v5
comparison has been run. Reduced explosion frequency or improved strategic
strength must be measured in fresh games, not inferred from the prompt change.
